00Executive Summary

The opportunity is real, the constraints are real, and the two need to be held together.

This document provides a grounded analysis of the Clinical Research/Operations vertical at Biomapas, examined specifically through the lens of where automation and AI augmentation can create measurable operational and commercial value — and where they cannot yet, and why. It is structured to support an informed leadership decision on whether establishing a Head of Research & Innovation function makes strategic sense, what that function would realistically own, and what the sequencing of work should be.

The core finding is straightforward. The global AI-based clinical trials solution market is valued at approximately $3.5 billion in 2026 and projected to reach $30 billion by 2034 at a CAGR of 30.9%. Large CROs — IQVIA, ICON, Thermo Fisher PPD — are already deploying agentic AI in production environments across protocol automation, safety operations, and central monitoring. Veeva Systems has scheduled AI agent releases across all clinical applications through 2026, including Vault Safety (April 2026), Clinical Operations and Regulatory (August 2026), and Clinical Data (December 2026). The industry is not waiting.

Biomapas's competitive position in this environment is specific: it is a specialist, regional CRO with genuine depth in CEE/EU markets, a strong early-phase oncology service line, and 20+ years of accumulated operational intelligence that constitutes a proprietary data asset no generalist platform can replicate quickly. That is the strategic foundation from which an innovation agenda must be built — not generic AI adoption, but targeted, legally compliant, operationally grounded capability-building that differentiates Biomapas in bid defense and improves margin per study.

The single structural reality that governs every AI initiative in this vertical: Biomapas operates as a Data Processor under GDPR, not a Data Controller. Every clinical data record it touches — eCRF, safety, imaging, PK/PD — is owned by the sponsor. Any AI use case that trains on or processes trial-specific patient data requires explicit DPA amendment with each sponsor individually. This is not a blocker, but it is a sequencing constraint that must be built into every commercial conversation, every vendor evaluation, and every internal build decision. It is also one of the most important things to communicate clearly to leadership — because AI vendors will not volunteer this complexity.

Part IWhat Is Actually Happening in the Market (May 2026)

The macro shift is already priced in.

The global CRO market was valued at approximately $72.5 billion in 2026 and is projected to reach $154.9 billion by 2033. Within that, the AI-specific clinical operations automation sub-segment is growing at 34.3% per year. By 2025, AI companies captured 55% of all health tech venture funding globally, up from 37% in 2024. Regulatory bodies including EMA and FDA are actively endorsing AI-powered solutions, with over 60% of clinical trial sponsors now exploring AI-based tools for data analysis and protocol design.

This is not speculative. Sponsors are already reweighting contract decisions: CROs that cannot demonstrate data-driven feasibility, automated compliance infrastructure, or AI-augmented delivery are being systematically de-prioritized in RFP scoring. Revised UK clinical trial regulations effective April 28, 2026 introduce new requirements including mandatory public registration and results publication within 12 months — increasing administrative burden and making automation economically necessary, not optional. The EU Biotech Act and Digital Omnibus simplification proposals are adding further regulatory momentum for technology-enabled trial operations.

Where large CROs are moving

IQVIA deployed over 50 AI agents in production environments in Q3 2025, improving its Clinical Program Productivity Index by 12% in 2024. ICON has integrated generative AI for protocol drafting and regulatory automation. Veeva Systems — the platform that Biomapas almost certainly uses for Safety, CTMS, and eTMF — announced AI agent availability across all Vault applications through 2026: Safety and Quality from April 2026, Clinical Operations and Regulatory from August 2026. Critically, Veeva AI is usage-based, meaning even a mid-size CRO like Biomapas can access production-grade agentic AI without enterprise-scale investment, provided its Vault configuration is current and its processes are clean enough to benefit from automation.

The strategic implication: the technology infrastructure to automate significant portions of the Clinical Research/Operations workflow is becoming a licensing decision, not a build decision — but only for organizations whose internal processes and data are sufficiently structured to make use of it. This is where the maturity gap often sits in specialty CROs.

The competitive threat in CEE

Large global CROs are not Biomapas's primary competitive threat in Poland, Romania, Czech Republic, or the Baltics. They are overpriced and too process-heavy for the mid-size biotech sponsors who anchor Biomapas's client base. The actual threat is a new category of AI-native boutique CROs specifically capitalizing on the mid-market gap in Europe. Companies like Lindus Health (UK, AI-native CRO model, Series A raised 2022–2024), and several Eastern European technology-adjacent CRO startups are building with AI as a core operating assumption rather than a retrofit. If Biomapas does not close the capability gap within 18–24 months, the positioning advantage in CEE becomes harder to defend on technology grounds — even if the site network and regulatory expertise remain genuinely superior.

Part IIProcess-Level Analysis — Clinical Research/Operations Vertical

Operationally the most complex service line Biomapas runs.

The Clinical Research/Operations vertical spans 12 service sub-lines — from Business Development and bid defense through Clinical Strategy, Study Start-Up, Early Phase Operations, Phase II–III Monitoring, Clinical Safety, Quality & Compliance, Post-Marketing, Medical Devices (MDR), and M&A Support — across a multi-jurisdiction geography covering EU-27, UK, Turkey, Georgia, and accessible CIS markets.

What the process map reveals about AI readiness

The existing process documentation is detailed and structurally sound. Three honest observations are worth naming before moving to opportunities:

  1. The process map is built from publicly available information and operational logic.

    It correctly identifies the major workflows, but the actual friction points — where manual effort is highest, where errors accumulate, where time is lost — can only be validated by the people who run these processes daily. CRAs, Safety Processors, Regulatory Affairs leads, and Data Managers will have specific views that no external mapping can fully anticipate. Any innovation agenda must include structured interviews with these functional leads before committing to an automation roadmap.

  2. The FSP model creates a structurally different data situation than the full-service CRO model.

    In FSP engagements, Biomapas staff work inside sponsor systems — meaning Biomapas has no proprietary data asset in that context at all. This limits the AI surfaces available for internal tooling and makes the innovation agenda more dependent on sponsor-level contract conversations than the full-service model.

  3. The geographic coverage stated commercially is broader than the operational revenue concentration.

    Based on public evidence and operational logic, the markets where Biomapas has genuine operational depth — established site networks, locally placed CRAs, active client portfolios — are Poland, France, Belgium, UK, Romania, Czech Republic, Slovakia, Bulgaria, the Baltics, the Netherlands, and Turkey. CIS markets (Kazakhstan, Ukraine, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Moldova) are strategically important and worth the investment in regulatory intelligence, but they are not yet at the scale that justifies primary technology investment. Any vendor selection, partnership model, or automation tool must be evaluated against the EU core + UK + Turkey geography first.

Part IIIWhere Automation and AI Can Create Real Value

A tiered roadmap, sequenced by GDPR exposure, technology maturity, and strategic value.

Tier 1

Compliance Automation — Non-Negotiable, Do It First

These are the highest-priority automation opportunities. They operate on rules and structural data, carry no GDPR complexity, and the compliance risk of not building them is materially higher than any implementation risk. They are also the most credible things to demonstrate to pharma sponsors in bid defense — because they show that Biomapas operates with real-time quality infrastructure rather than periodic manual oversight.

SUSAR Multi-Jurisdiction Routing Engine

Routing SUSAR submissions across EudraVigilance (EU-27 via E2B R3), MHRA UK E2B gateway, TİTCK EBS (Turkey), and national MoH systems in CIS markets is currently manual coordination. Country-specific reporting timelines (Day 7 fatal, Day 15 non-fatal across all active markets) are fixed and known. A rules engine that auto-routes each case to the correct submission gateway based on case classification, study country authorization, and reporting timeline — with automated deadline alerts and submission confirmation logging — eliminates the single highest-risk compliance failure in the entire vertical. This is not an AI problem. It is a workflow automation problem with a well-defined solution.

Estimated FTE impact: 30–40% of safety operations administrative overhead

Real-Time eTMF Completeness Scoring

The DIA eTMF Reference Model defines exactly which documents must be present at each study milestone. An automated completeness-scoring engine — cross-referencing expected documents against actual eTMF population, updated continuously — transforms inspection readiness from a periodic audit event into a live operational metric. Veeva Vault eTMF already provides the platform substrate; the question is whether the business rules layer is configured at study-specific granularity or left at generic level. Biomapas can offer sponsors a real-time eTMF health score as a bid defense differentiator. No mid-size CEE competitor currently does this as standard.

Regulatory Calendar Automation

EU CTIS (60–90 days), MHRA (30 days), TİTCK (45–60 days since the November 2025 QP Declaration equivalence update), Georgia MoH, Kazakhstan MoH — all have known authorization timelines with defined RFI windows, amendment notification requirements, and condition closure deadlines. An automated country clock tool that tracks all active regulatory submissions and flags upcoming deadlines removes one of the most common causes of study start-up slippage: intermediate deadline drift due to manual calendar management. Build internally first; productize as a sponsor-facing transparency dashboard within 12–18 months.

SAE/EDC Automated Reconciliation

The reconciliation between Vault Safety and EDC platforms (Medidata Rave, Oracle Clinical One, Veeva EDC) is a known integration problem with known solutions. Medidata's platform offers partial native reconciliation; Veeva's AI agent layer (April 2026 release for Safety) will extend this further. The decision point for Biomapas is whether to rely on vendor-native tooling or build a custom cross-platform reconciliation layer capable of working across the multiple EDC systems encountered in multi-sponsor FSP engagements. If FSP represents more than 40% of revenue, a cross-platform approach becomes a genuine internal asset.

Tier 2

AI-Assisted Processes — License First, Build Only Where Proprietary Data Creates Advantage

MedDRA Auto-Coding

NLP models trained on MedDRA terminology hierarchies now achieve 70–80% auto-coding accuracy on adverse event narratives at commercial maturity. Veeva Vault Safety already includes MedDRA auto-coding functionality (available since the 20R2 release). Saama Technologies, which won Best AI-Based Solution for Life Sciences in 2024, offers modular AI agents directly applicable to ICSR coding workflows. Veeva's 2026 Clinical Data Trend Report identifies medical coding as the clearest near-term winner for AI augmentation. The correct Biomapas move is to activate this within the existing Vault configuration and measure auto-coding throughput before evaluating additional vendors.

At 500+ cases per month portfolio-wide → significant recovered Medical Coder capacity

ICSR Narrative Auto-Generation

The structured data already in Vault Safety — patient demographics, event description, suspect product, concomitant medications, medical history — is sufficient to generate a regulatory-quality ICSR narrative draft using an LLM with constrained output format. The draft requires Medical Monitor QC review before submission, but the generation step (currently 45–60 minutes per case at specialist rates) can be reduced to a 10–15 minute review workflow. At portfolio scale, this is a 60–70% reduction in per-case narrative production time. Veeva's emerging AI layer within Vault Safety and Saama's Clinical AI Agents are the primary platforms to evaluate. This is a licensing decision, not a build candidate.

Monitoring Visit Report (MVR) Auto-Generation

CRAs currently spend 3–5 hours per monitoring visit composing a structured MVR from visit notes and EDC observations. An LLM-based drafting tool that ingests a structured digital visit checklist — EDC query status, deviations observed, IMP accountability, consent binder status — and generates a draft report reduces this to a review-and-approve workflow. Across a portfolio of 50 active sites visited every 6–8 weeks, this represents approximately 2,000 CRA-hours per year in recovered capacity — equivalent to roughly €120,000–€180,000 in redeployable CRA time without additional headcount, at CEE market rates. Within reach via Veeva AI (August 2026 release for Clinical Operations) or a custom LLM integration against a structured template.

Protocol Consistency Checker

A protocol contains internal logical dependencies — inclusion/exclusion criteria must align with primary endpoints; the schedule of assessments must be consistent with the statistical analysis plan; prohibited medications must be consistent with indication-specific standards of care. An NLP cross-check engine that flags inconsistencies before CTA submission prevents expensive protocol amendments. Typical amendment cost in EU clinical trials: €50,000–€200,000 per study, including regulatory fees, re-submission timelines, and clinical delays. Biomapas's corpus of previously authored protocols — if systematically catalogued and cleaned — provides training material that creates a proprietary advantage over a generic off-the-shelf tool. Tier 2 build candidate over 12–18 months, contingent on confirming a structured protocol archive.

Enrollment Prediction Model — The Proprietary Asset

The underutilized data asset in the entire operation is the internal site performance history: enrollment rates, screen failure ratios, monitoring findings, and deviation patterns accumulated across 20+ years of CEE and CIS site operations. An ML model trained on this data — combined with public data from ClinicalTrials.gov, CTIS, and TrialHub benchmarking — generates enrollment timeline predictions in bid defense that are validated against actual historical performance, not estimated from public benchmarks. This is a competitive differentiator that no CEE-regional competitor can replicate without equivalent site network depth. The key question is whether this data exists in structured, queryable form (extractable from CTMS/eTMF) or in unstructured form. If the latter is true, the first investment is data structuring and ETL architecture, not model building.

Tier 3

Advanced AI — Partnership and Investment Logic

EHR Pre-Screening at Anchor Sites

Patient recruitment delays account for approximately 30% of total clinical trial timelines. AI pre-screening of EHR data against protocol eligibility criteria — identifying eligible patients before a research coordinator manually reviews charts — is now commercially deployed at scale. ConcertAI and Deep6.ai are the leading players; both are expanding EU operations in 2025–2026. The access constraint for a CRO is real: hospital EHR data is held by the institution, not the CRO. The solution path is a formal data access agreement with 3–5 anchor sites in EU countries — the markets with the deepest existing site relationships — granting read access to de-identified EHR data under GDPR-compliant research frameworks. This is a 12–18 month legal and compliance project before any technology deployment. But if executed, it transforms Biomapas's enrollment credibility from historical rate benchmarking to real-time patient pool sizing.

Digital Twins / Synthetic Control Arms

Unlearn.ai (funded at $130M+, backed by Altimeter Capital and Insight Partners, EMA positive opinion on TwinRCT™ methodology, active Merck KGaA partnership) is the market leader in generative AI for synthetic control arms. Their technology allows Phase II/III trials to reduce control arm size by generating AI-predicted placebo outcomes — directly reducing patient burden and trial cost. For Biomapas, the most pragmatic engagement model is a commercial referral or co-marketing arrangement: Biomapas, as the managing CRO for Phase II/III oncology studies in CEE, recommends or integrates Unlearn's methodology to sponsors seeking adaptive designs. This differentiates Biomapas in oncology bid defense, particularly in markets like Poland and Czech Republic where patient pools are limited and enrollment competition is high. A formal partnership conversation with Unlearn is worth initiating.

RWE / PASS Study Data Infrastructure

Post-Marketing (PASS, PAES, non-interventional studies) is a growing service line. The long-term trajectory of this service is secondary use of real-world EHR data for observational study design, not primary data collection at investigative sites. France's Health Data Hub and UK NHS Digital provide structured EHR access frameworks for approved research. In France — a high-revenue Biomapas market — SNDS (Système National des Données de Santé) access for authorized research organizations is an established pathway. A 24-month strategic project to qualify Biomapas as an authorized data user in France and UK unlocks new PASS study capability that competitors without established regulatory presence cannot easily access. Potential technology partners: TriNetX, Aetion, Flatiron Health.

Part VIWhat Needs to Be Understood Before the Workshop

The workshop is not a formatting exercise — it is where the assumptions get tested.

The process mapping prepared to date covers the external-facing process architecture of the Clinical Research/Operations vertical with sufficient depth to identify automation and AI insertion points. What it cannot do — and what no external analysis can do — is validate the internal operational reality.

  1. Data state of internal operational assets

    Is enrollment performance history by site, by indication, by country queryable from current CTMS? In what format does the protocol archive exist? Is monitoring visit history (deviations, queries, findings) structured or unstructured?

  2. FSP vs. full-service split

    What proportion of Clinical Research/Operations revenue comes from FSP engagements? This determines the degree to which Biomapas controls the systems and data architecture that AI initiatives depend on.

  3. Current Veeva configuration maturity

    Which Vault modules are live? Is the eTMF implementation using current DIA reference model mapping? Are AI agent tiers already contracted or not yet activated?

  4. Existing sponsor DPA language

    Do current MSA templates include any provision for anonymized data use for internal quality improvement or product development? If not, this needs to be a new standard clause — and it is a commercial conversation, not just a legal one.

  5. The CIS revenue reality

    What proportion of active studies currently run in Kazakhstan, Ukraine, or Armenia? Understanding the actual revenue weight in CIS markets is important for prioritizing the regulatory intelligence and CRA capacity investments correctly.

These questions cannot be answered in a one-day workshop that starts from a blank page. The process mapping done to date means the workshop can start from a structured hypothesis set — which services have the highest automation potential, which data assets are genuinely Biomapas-owned and usable, which vendor relationships to activate first — and use the session to validate or challenge those hypotheses against internal operational reality.

Part VIIHonest Assessment of the €200M Revenue Target

Three pathways, three time horizons, three certainty levels.

Biomapas has set a revenue target of €200M by 2030. In the context of the Clinical Research/Operations vertical alone, AI augmentation and innovation contribute to that target in three distinct ways — each with different time horizons and certainty levels.

Highest certainty · Near-term

Margin improvement through automation

SUSAR routing engines, eTMF completeness scoring, MVR auto-generation, and MedDRA auto-coding together can recover meaningful FTE capacity without headcount growth. This does not directly increase revenue, but it improves margin per study and allows the same team to absorb more studies — the operative growth mechanism for a specialty CRO.

Medium certainty · 12–24 months

Win rate improvement through data-driven bid defense

An enrollment prediction model built on internal site performance data, combined with TrialHub integration at the API level, creates a genuinely differentiated bid defense narrative — one that Biomapas can show sponsors with historical validation rather than estimated benchmarks. For a company running 20–30 bid defenses per year, improving win rate by 10–15 percentage points has a direct revenue consequence.

Longest horizon · Highest uncertainty

New revenue streams through product and partnership

PASS/RWE data services, EHR pre-screening partnerships at CEE anchor sites, and potential Unlearn.ai co-marketing for adaptive oncology designs represent revenue expansion opportunities — but require 18–36 months of legal, partnership, and technology groundwork. Real opportunities, but value-creating optionality, not the core path.

The €200M target is most credibly supported by a combination of: deepening the EU core market position (Poland, France, Belgium, UK, Romania, Czech Republic) using AI-enabled operational efficiency as a differentiator; defending and growing the CEE and Turkey early-phase oncology pipeline where Biomapas has genuine depth; and selectively adding capability in post-marketing and RWE services where the regulatory expertise already exists and the market is growing. Innovation investment should be calibrated to accelerate that strategy — not replace it with a technology-first narrative that the operational infrastructure is not yet ready to deliver.

ConclusionThe Honest Case for the Role

Not a technology-buying exercise — a multi-year capability program.

The Head of Research & Innovation function makes sense for Biomapas if — and only if — leadership is prepared for what building this capability actually requires. It is not a technology buying exercise. It is a multi-year program that begins with data governance and internal process validation, moves through targeted automation and vendor integration, and reaches advanced AI applications only when the foundational legal and operational infrastructure supports them.

The value this role creates is measurable: faster, more defensible bid defense, lower per-study operational cost, higher inspection readiness, and — over time — proprietary data products that competitors cannot easily replicate. What it requires from leadership is patience with the sequencing, willingness to have DPA and data governance conversations with sponsors, and realistic timelines that reflect the GDPR-constrained operating environment.

The opportunity is not to become an AI company. It is to become the most credible specialist CRO in CEE and broader Europe — one that can prove its operational claims with data. That is a commercially realistic ambition, and it is achievable within the 2030 horizon if the work starts now.
